Module 01

The Sun: The Solar Engine

The Sun is a G-type main-sequence star comprising 99.86% of the solar system's mass. Through the proton-proton chain nuclear fusion process, it fuses 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium every second in its core, generating light, thermal radiation, and the solar wind that shields our solar system within the heliosphere.

Module 02

Rotation vs. Revolution

Rotation (Axial Spin)

The turning of a planet on its internal axis. Determines the duration of day and night. Earth rotates once every 23.93 hours.

Revolution (Orbital Flight)

The travel of a planet along an elliptical orbit around the Sun. Determines planetary years. Earth revolves once every 365.25 days.

Module 03

Inner Planets vs. Outer Planets

Terrestrial (Inner)

Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. Compact, dense rocky surfaces, high metal content, few or no moons, no ring systems, and located inside the Asteroid Belt.

Jovian (Outer Giants)

Jupiter, Saturn (Gas Giants); Uranus, Neptune (Ice Giants). Massive, low-density hydrogen, helium, and icy volatile compositions, dozens of moons, and extensive ring systems.

Module 04

Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

Planets do not travel in perfect circles. According to Johannes Kepler:

  1. Planetary orbits are ellipses with the Sun sitting at one focal point.
  2. A line connecting a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time (planets move faster when closest to the Sun).
  3. The square of a planet's orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit ($P^2 = a^3$).

About Solar System Explorer

Exhibition project overview, design methodology, and pedagogical goals.

Project Vision

The Solar System Explorer was built for STEM science exhibitions to bridge the gap between static textbook diagrams and dynamic three-dimensional astrophysics. By combining spatial computing concepts with interactive gamification, students transition from passive observers to active cosmic researchers.

Pedagogical Benefits

  • Spatial Intuition: Visualizing scaled orbits demonstrates Keplerian speed variations directly.
  • Comparative Planetology: Immediate side-by-side metric inspection sharpens data literacy.
  • Immediate Knowledge Reinforcement: The STEM quiz rewards curiosity with contextual explanations.

Technical Architecture

HTML5

Semantic document layout, WebGL mounting frame, and accessible DOM structures.

CSS3

Modern dark-navy space theme, CSS glassmorphism, responsive grid layouts, and custom scrollbars.

Three.js (WebGL)

Hardware-accelerated 3D scene rendering, procedural planetary textures, point-light illumination, and orbital mathematics.

Vanilla JavaScript

Modular, dependency-free architecture featuring smooth raycasting interactions, state management, and quiz computation.
