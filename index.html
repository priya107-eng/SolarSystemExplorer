STEM Educational Concepts

Core principles of celestial mechanics, structural classification, and astrophysics.

Module 01

The Sun: The Solar Engine

The Sun is a G-type main-sequence star comprising 99.86% of the solar system's mass. Through nuclear fusion, it converts 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium every second in its core, producing light and thermal radiation.

Module 02

Rotation vs. Revolution

Rotation (Axial Spin)

The turning of a planet on its internal axis. Determines day and night. Earth rotates once every 23.93 hours.

Revolution (Orbital Flight)

The travel of a planet along an elliptical orbit around the Sun. Determines years. Earth revolves once every 365.25 days.

Module 03

Inner Planets vs. Outer Planets

Terrestrial (Inner)

Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. Rocky surfaces, high density, metal cores, few or no moons, and zero ring systems.

Jovian (Outer Giants)

Jupiter, Saturn (Gas Giants); Uranus, Neptune (Ice Giants). Massive, low-density hydrogen/methane compositions, ring systems, and many moons.

Module 04

Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

  1. Planetary orbits are ellipses with the Sun at one focal point.
  2. A line connecting a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time.
  3. The square of a planet's orbital period is proportional to the cube of its semi-major axis ($P^2 = a^3$).
Module 05

Asteroid Belt vs. Kuiper Belt

Main Asteroid Belt

Located between Mars and Jupiter. Composed of millions of rocky bodies and metal fragments, including the dwarf planet Ceres.

Kuiper Belt & Oort Cloud

Extending beyond Neptune. A realm of frozen volatiles (methane, ammonia, water ice), home to Pluto and short-period comets.

Module 06

Classification & Lifecycle of Stars

  • Red Dwarfs: Cool, long-lived stars (Proxima Centauri). Over 70% of stars in our galaxy are red dwarfs.
  • Yellow Dwarfs (G-Type): Intermediate mass and temperature stars like our Sun, lasting ~10 billion years.
  • Blue Giants / Supergiants: High surface temperature, massive, fast-burning stars that end in Supernovae.
  • White Dwarfs: Dense stellar remnants remaining after lower-mass stars shed their outer envelopes.
Module 07

Galaxies & The Milky Way

Our Solar System is located on the Orion Arm of the Milky Way—a barred spiral galaxy spanning ~100,000 light-years.

  • The galactic center hosts Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole.
  • The closest major spiral galaxy neighbor to the Milky Way is the Andromeda Galaxy (M31).
Module 08

Natural vs. Artificial Satellites

Natural Satellites (Moons)

Gravitationally bound celestial bodies orbiting larger planets (e.g., Earth's Moon, Ganymede, Titan).

Artificial Satellites

Human-made spacecraft placed into orbit for telecommunications, weather monitoring, and science (e.g., ISS, Hubble, GPS).

About Solar System Explorer

Exhibition project overview, design methodology, and pedagogical goals.

Project Vision

The Solar System Explorer was built for STEM science exhibitions to bridge the gap between static textbook diagrams and dynamic three-dimensional astrophysics. By combining spatial computing concepts with interactive gamification, students transition from passive observers to active cosmic researchers.

Pedagogical Benefits

  • Spatial Intuition: Visualizing scaled orbits demonstrates Keplerian speed variations directly.
  • Comprehensive Cosmogony: Beyond planets, the scene introduces asteroidal belts, planetary rings, cometary dynamics, and artificial satellites.
  • Immediate Knowledge Reinforcement: The STEM quiz rewards curiosity with contextual explanations.

Technical Architecture

HTML5

Semantic document layout, WebGL mounting frame, and accessible DOM structures.

CSS3

Modern dark-navy space theme, CSS glassmorphism, responsive grid layouts, and custom scrollbars.

Three.js (r128)

Hardware-accelerated 3D scene rendering, procedural planetary textures, point-light illumination, and orbital mathematics.

Vanilla JavaScript

Modular, dependency-free architecture featuring smooth raycasting interactions, state management, and quiz computation.
